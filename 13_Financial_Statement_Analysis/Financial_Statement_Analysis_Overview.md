# Financial Statement Analysis — Overview

Six modules. Intercorporate investments, multinational translation, and pensions are the highest-yield, most computational; quality-of-reports and integration are conceptual.

## Module 7 — Intercorporate Investments → [[Intercorporate_Investments]]
Financial assets (FVPL/FVOCI/amortized cost), equity method (associates/JVs), acquisition method (consolidation, NCI, full vs partial goodwill).

## Module 8 — Employee Compensation → [[Employee_Compensation]]
Share-based comp (fair value at grant, vesting); DB pensions (funded status = plan assets − PBO; IFRS vs US GAAP P&L/OCI split; expected vs discount-rate return).

## Module 9 — Multinational Operations → [[Multinational_Operations]]
Current rate (translation, CTA in equity) vs temporal (remeasurement, gain/loss in NI); exposure; hyperinflation; tax-rate effects.

## Module 10 — Analysis of Financial Institutions → [[Analysis_of_Financial_Institutions]]
Why banks differ; Basel III (CET1, LCR, NSFR); **CAMELS**; insurer combined ratio.

## Module 11 — Quality of Financial Reports → [[Quality_of_Financial_Reports]]
Quality spectrum; earnings persistence and accruals; mean reversion; cash-flow/balance-sheet quality; Beneish M-score.

## Module 12 — Integration of FSA Techniques → [[Integration_of_FSA_Techniques]]
Six-step framework; extended DuPont (remove associate equity income/investment); asset base & goodwill; burdensome vs non-burdensome leverage; segment capital allocation (CapEx%/Assets%); accruals ratio (balance-sheet & cash-flow) and earnings quality; CGO; market-value decomposition (implied standalone P/E).

## Cross-cutting Exam Traps
- Equity method vs consolidation → same net income, different ratios.
- Translation gain → equity (CTA); remeasurement gain → net income.
- Funded status = plan assets − PBO; US GAAP pension expense uses expected return.
- Reporting quality ≠ earnings quality; high accruals = low quality + faster mean reversion.
