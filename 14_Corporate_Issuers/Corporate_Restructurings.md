# Corporate Restructurings

## Types & Motivations (16.a)
- **Investment / expansion**: equity investments, joint ventures, **acquisitions** (horizontal,
  vertical, conglomerate).
- **Divestment / contraction**: asset/segment **sale**, **spin-off** (existing holders get new-entity
  shares pro-rata), **split-off** (exchange parent shares for subsidiary shares), **equity carve-out**
  (IPO a stake to outsiders for cash).
- **Restructuring**: **cost** restructuring (cut costs), **balance-sheet** restructuring (change asset
  or capital mix, recapitalization, leveraged recap).
- Motivations: synergies, growth, scale/scope, undervaluation, focus (divest non-core), tax, financial.

## Initial Evaluation (16.b)
A restructuring matters to analysis when it is **material** (size relative to the firm), **changes the
business mix/risk**, or is **non-routine**. Frame around: what, why, when, and the financial impact.

## Valuation Methods (16.c)
| Method | Basis | Notes |
|------|------|------|
| **Comparable company analysis** | Trading multiples of peers | Add an estimated **takeover premium** |
| **Comparable transaction analysis** | Multiples paid in similar deals | Premium already embedded |
| **Premium-paid / DCF** | DCF of target + synergies | Sensitive to assumptions |

- **Takeover premium**: `premium = (DP − UP) / UP`, where DP = deal price, UP = unaffected
  (pre-announcement) price. Use a price from before rumors leaked (e.g. a week prior or VWAP) for UP.

## Effects on EPS, Net Debt/EBITDA, WACC (16.d)
- **EPS**: depends on financing and relative P/Es. Stock-financed deal where acquirer P/E **> ** target
  P/E → typically **accretive**; cash/debt-financed → compare earnings yield to after-tax cost of debt.
- **Net debt / EBITDA**: debt-financed deals raise net debt and leverage; assess against covenants.
- **WACC**: changes with the post-deal capital structure and combined business risk.

## Investment vs Divestment Actions (16.e, 16.f, 16.g)
- **Equity investments / JVs / acquisitions**: evaluate strategic fit, control, accounting method, and
  value creation (synergies − premium).
- **Divestitures (sale / spin-off)**: can unlock value by removing **conglomerate discounts**,
  improving focus and capital allocation.
- **Cost & balance-sheet restructurings**: improve margins or rebalance leverage; watch one-off charges
  and earnings-quality effects.

## Exam Traps
- **Spin-off** = pro-rata new shares, no cash to parent; **carve-out** = sell a stake via IPO for cash;
  **split-off** = holders swap parent shares for subsidiary shares.
- Takeover premium uses the **pre-announcement (unaffected)** price as the denominator.
- Stock-financed accretion/dilution hinges on the **relative P/E** of acquirer vs target.

## Q&A

### 2026-06-03 — Spin-off vs split-off vs equity carve-out
**Q:** Distinguish the three main divestiture forms and which raise cash for the parent.
**A:** **Spin-off** — existing shareholders receive **pro-rata shares** of the new standalone entity; **no
cash** to the parent. **Split-off** — shareholders **exchange** some parent shares for subsidiary shares
(reduces parent share count); no cash raised. **Equity carve-out** — parent **sells a stake via IPO** to
outside investors → **raises cash**. All three can unlock value by reducing a conglomerate discount and
improving focus. Trap: only the carve-out (and an outright asset sale) brings cash into the parent.

### 2026-06-03 — Computing the takeover premium
**Q:** A target trades at $30 a week before any rumor; the deal is announced at $42. What's the premium?
**A:** `Premium = (DP − UP) / UP = (42 − 30)/30 = 40%`, where DP = deal price and UP = **unaffected**
(pre-announcement) price. Always use a price from **before** rumors leaked (e.g., a week prior or a VWAP),
not the price right before announcement, which already reflects speculation. Comparable-**transaction**
multiples already embed a premium; comparable-**company** multiples require adding an estimated premium.
