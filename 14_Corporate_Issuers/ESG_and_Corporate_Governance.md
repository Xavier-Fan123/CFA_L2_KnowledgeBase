# ESG and Corporate Governance

## Ownership Structures (14.a)
- **Dispersed** ownership (many small holders) → **principal-agent** (owner-manager) conflict; weak
  individual monitoring.
- **Concentrated** ownership (controlling shareholder) → **principal-principal** conflict (controlling
  vs minority shareholders). Can be horizontal (among large holders) or vertical (controlling vs
  minority).
- Mechanisms: **dual-class shares**, voting caps, pyramids, cross-holdings — separate **control from
  cash-flow rights**, entrenching insiders.

## Evaluating Governance Effectiveness (14.b)
- Board: independence, expertise, separation of chair/CEO, committee structure (audit, remuneration,
  nomination), staggered vs annual elections.
- Shareholder rights: voting (one-share-one-vote vs dual class), takeover defenses, related-party
  transactions.
- Remuneration alignment; transparency and audit quality.

## ESG Integration (14.c, 14.d)
- **Materiality**: focus on ESG factors **financially material** to the issuer/industry (e.g.,
  emissions for utilities, data privacy for tech).
- Implementation approaches: **negative/exclusionary screening**, **positive/best-in-class screening**,
  **thematic** investing, **ESG integration** (into forecasts/valuation), **engagement/active
  ownership**, **impact** investing.
- Analytical use: adjust revenue/margins, capex, cost of capital, and terminal assumptions for ESG
  risks (stranded assets, litigation, regulatory) and opportunities.

## Exam Traps
- Dispersed ownership → **principal-agent**; concentrated ownership → **principal-principal** (minority
  expropriation) conflict.
- Dual-class structures separate **control rights from cash-flow rights** — a governance red flag.
- ESG analysis should center on **financially material** factors, not all ESG issues.

## Q&A

### 2026-06-03 — Principal-agent vs principal-principal conflict
**Q:** How does ownership structure determine the type of governance conflict?
**A:** **Dispersed ownership** (many small holders) → **principal-agent** conflict: owners (principals) vs
managers (agents), with weak monitoring because no single holder has incentive to police management.
**Concentrated ownership** (a controlling shareholder) → **principal-principal** conflict: the controlling
shareholder vs **minority** shareholders, who can be expropriated. Mechanisms like **dual-class shares,
pyramids, and cross-holdings** separate **control rights from cash-flow rights**, entrenching insiders — a
governance red flag.
Related: [[Corporate_Restructurings]]

### 2026-06-03 — What does "materiality" mean in ESG integration?
**Q:** Should an analyst weigh all ESG issues equally?
**A:** No — focus on factors that are **financially material** to that issuer/industry (e.g., carbon
emissions for utilities, data privacy for tech, water for beverages). Material ESG risks feed into
forecasts: adjust revenue/margins, capex, **cost of capital**, and terminal assumptions for risks
(stranded assets, litigation, regulation) and opportunities. Implementation styles range from
negative/positive screening and thematic/impact investing to full **ESG integration** and active ownership.
