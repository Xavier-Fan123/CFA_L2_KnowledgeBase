# Equity Valuation: Applications and Processes

## Value Concepts (17.a, 17.b, 17.c)
- **Intrinsic value**: the "true" value given complete information; analysts profit from **perceived
  mispricing** = (intrinsic − market price). Requires that the market eventually corrects.
- `Perceived mispricing = (true mispricing) + (error in the estimate of intrinsic value)`.
- **Going-concern** value (operating indefinitely) vs **liquidation** value (cease operations, sell
  assets). Most relevant for public companies: **fair (market) value** under the going-concern premise.

## Applications (17.d)
Stock selection, inferring market expectations, evaluating corporate events (M&A, IPOs), fairness
opinions, research, planning, communicating with management.

## Industry & Competitive Analysis (17.e)
Address: industry structure (Porter's five forces), demand/supply, life-cycle stage, profitability
drivers, competitive advantage and its sustainability, and the firm's strategy.

## Model Types (17.f, 17.g, 17.h)
- **Absolute valuation**: estimates intrinsic value directly — DDM, FCF, residual income, asset-based.
- **Relative valuation**: value relative to peers via **multiples** (method of comparables).
- **Sum-of-the-parts**: value each business segment separately and add; a **conglomerate discount** may
  apply (diversified firms can trade below the sum of parts due to inefficiency, opacity, or
  over-investment).
- **Model selection** criteria: consistent with firm characteristics (dividends? positive FCF? quality
  accounting?), purpose, and data availability.

## Exam Traps
- Analyst edge = **perceived** mispricing, which includes estimation error in intrinsic value.
- **Sum-of-the-parts** can reveal a **conglomerate discount**.
- Choose the model that fits the firm: non-payer with negative FCF but clean accounting → residual income.

## Q&A

### 2026-06-03 — Perceived vs true mispricing
**Q:** Why is the analyst's edge "perceived" mispricing, and what risks does that hide?
**A:** `Perceived mispricing = true mispricing + error in your estimate of intrinsic value`. You only ever
observe market price and **your** intrinsic-value estimate, so apparent mispricing blends a real
opportunity with your own estimation error. Two extra conditions must hold to profit: (1) the market
must **eventually converge** to intrinsic value, and (2) you need the right **catalyst/horizon**. A
confident but wrong intrinsic estimate produces false "mispricing."

### 2026-06-03 — Choosing a valuation model
**Q:** How do you pick among DDM, FCF, residual income, and multiples?
**A:** Match the model to the firm: **DDM** for stable dividend payers (minority view); **FCFE/FCFF** for
firms with positive, forecastable free cash flow or a control perspective; **residual income** for
non-payers with negative FCF but **high-quality accounting** and uncertain terminal value; **multiples**
for quick relative views and when peers are comparable; **asset-based / sum-of-the-parts** for holding
companies or to surface a conglomerate discount. Also weigh purpose and data availability.
