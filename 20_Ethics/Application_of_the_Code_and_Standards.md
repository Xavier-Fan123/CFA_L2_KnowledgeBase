# Application of the Code and Standards: Level II

## Overview
This is the **distinctive Level II ethics reading** — it is **case-based**, not new theory. The Code and Standards are the same as Level I; here you **apply** them to multi-paragraph fact patterns. The official volume presents **seven cases** set in private and institutional asset management. Each case maps facts to one or more Standards.

**LOS (42.a-42.b):**
- **Evaluate** practices, policies, and conduct relative to the Code and Standards.
- **Explain** how the practices, policies, and conduct **do or do not** violate the Code and Standards.

## The Ethical Decision-Making Framework (memorize the 4 steps)
*(Official V10, LM3, Exhibit 1.)* The components need not be done in strict order, but **end with a review.**

| Step | What you do |
|------|-------------|
| **1. Identify** | Relevant **facts**, **stakeholders & duties owed**, **ethical principles**, and **conflicts of interest**. |
| **2. Consider** | **Situational influences** (e.g., pressure, incentives), **additional guidance** (firm policy, legal counsel, the Standards), and **alternative actions**. |
| **3. Decide and act** | Choose and take the action that best satisfies the Code and Standards (the **highest** standard). |
| **4. Reflect** | Was the outcome as anticipated? Why or why not? Use the insight to improve future decisions. |

Mnemonic: **I-C-D-R** (Identify → Consider → Decide/act → Reflect).

## How the Seven Cases Are Structured
- **First five cases → "identify" only.** Determine **whether** a violation occurred and **explain why / why not**.
- **Last two cases → "identify + remediate."** Identify violations, state the **corrective actions required**, and draft a **policy statement a firm could adopt** to prevent recurrence.

The seven cases (official V10, LM3):
1. **Serengeti Advisory Services** — equity research firm; premium-tier service, undisclosed family ownership of a recommended company, an "approved list" of third-party research, plagiarism/attribution. Implicates **I(C) Misrepresentation**, **III(B) Fair Dealing**, **VI(A) Avoid or Disclose Conflicts**, **V(A) Diligence**.
2. **Banco Libertad** — bank/asset-management conflicts, suitability and loyalty issues (**III(A)/III(C)**, **VI(A)**).
3. **QuantHouse** — quantitative model research and reasonable-basis/diligence questions (**V(A)**, model vetting).
4. **Jacobs, Riccio, and Associates** — communication, supervision, and conflicts (**IV(C)**, **V(B)**, **VI**).
5. **Magadi Asset Management** — duties to clients/employer; fair dealing and priority of transactions (**III/IV/VI**).
6. **Syyark** *(action + policy)* — adviser recommends an unfamiliar asset (cryptocurrency) before becoming proficient. Implicates **I(E) Competence**, **V(A) Diligence**, **III(C) Suitability**, and conflict-of-interest disclosure (**VI(A)**). Remedy: gain competence (CE/training/certification) before recommending; firm policy requiring proficiency in any new product before it is offered.
7. **Agarway** *(action + policy)* — crowdfunding/listing platform due diligence under founder pressure, plus personal investments in companies being evaluated and information-based promotion. Implicates **I(B) Independence & Objectivity**, **II(B) Market Manipulation**, and **VI(A) Avoid or Disclose Conflicts**.

> Note: the published case write-ups reference editions of the Handbook (e.g., the Serengeti case is from the 11th edition); the L2 2026 curriculum has integrated them and the **2024 revisions apply** — most importantly, the Syyark case turns on the **new I(E) Competence** standard and conflicts are framed under **VI(A) "Avoid or Disclose."**

## How to Answer an Application Item (exam method)
1. **Spot every Standard** the facts trigger — items reward identifying **all** applicable Standards, not just the most obvious one. A single sentence can breach several (e.g., recommending an unfamiliar, high-risk asset to a subset of clients hits I(E) **and** V(A) **and** III(C) **and** III(B)).
2. State **violation vs. no violation** and the **reason**, citing the specific subsection (e.g., "violates III(C) because suitability was not assessed against the client's total portfolio").
3. For action/policy items, give the **required corrective action** (divest, disclose, dissociate, increase supervision, gain competence) **and** a concrete **firm policy statement** that would prevent recurrence.
4. Always default to the **highest standard of conduct** and the **stricter** of law vs. the Code.

## Exam Traps
- This reading adds **no new rules** — losing points here means failing to **apply** known Standards, not lack of theory.
- Identify **all** Standards implicated; partial identification loses credit.
- Don't confuse the two case types: the **last two** cases also need **corrective actions + a firm policy**, not just a yes/no.
- Use the **2024** subsection names: **I(E) Competence** exists; **VI(A)** is **"Avoid or Disclose Conflicts."**
- "No violation" is sometimes the correct answer (e.g., conduct that merely *looks* like a conflict but is fully disclosed and managed) — read for whether the member **disclosed/avoided** appropriately before concluding a breach.

## Q&A

### 2026-06-04 — What does the Level II "Application" ethics reading actually test?
**Q:** How is the Level II Application of the Code and Standards reading different from Level I ethics, and what is the framework?
**A:** It is purely **case-based application** — the Standards are identical to Level I, but you must read rich, multi-paragraph scenarios and decide **whether** each practice violates a Standard (and, in the last two of the seven cases, prescribe **corrective actions + a firm policy statement**). The official **ethical decision-making framework** has four steps: **Identify** (facts, stakeholders/duties, principles, conflicts) → **Consider** (situational influences, additional guidance, alternatives) → **Decide and act** → **Reflect** (was the outcome as expected?). Exam method: name **every** Standard the facts trigger, state violation/no-violation with the subsection cited, then give the required action and a preventive policy. Default to the **highest** standard and the **stricter** of law vs. Code.

### 2026-06-04 — "First five vs. last two" cases — why the distinction matters
**Q:** The Application reading splits its seven cases into two groups. What's the difference, and why does it matter on the exam?
**A:** The **first five cases** ask only whether a **violation occurred** and **why/why not**. The **last two cases** (Syyark and Agarway) go further: you must **identify violations, specify the corrective actions required, and draft a policy statement** a firm could use to prevent recurrence. It matters because an item drawn from the last two cases that only asks "did X violate a Standard?" is incomplete — the curriculum expects you to also know the **remedy and the firm-level control**. Syyark turns on the **new I(E) Competence** standard (recommending crypto before becoming proficient); Agarway turns on **I(B) Independence/Objectivity, II(B) Market Manipulation, and VI(A) Avoid or Disclose Conflicts** (founder pressure on due diligence, paid promotion, and personal stakes in companies under review).
