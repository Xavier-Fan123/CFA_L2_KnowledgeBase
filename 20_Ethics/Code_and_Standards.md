# Code of Ethics and Standards of Professional Conduct

At Level II, ethics is **application/case-based** — the Standards are nearly identical to Level I; the skill is
applying them to nuanced fact patterns. Always pick the answer that requires the **highest standard of
conduct**. The exam uses the **Standards of Practice Handbook reflecting the 2023/2024 revisions** (effective with
the 2024 curriculum). Watch the three changes from older editions (see "2024 Revisions" below).

## The Six Components of the Code of Ethics
Members and Candidates must:
1. **Act with integrity, competence, diligence, and respect** and in an ethical manner with the public, clients,
   prospective clients, employers, employees, colleagues, and other capital-market participants.
2. Place the **integrity of the profession and the interests of clients above their own** personal interests.
3. Use **reasonable care and independent professional judgment** in analysis, recommendations, actions.
4. **Practice and encourage others** to practice in a professional and ethical manner reflecting credit on the profession.
5. **Promote the integrity and viability of the global capital markets** for the ultimate benefit of society.
6. **Maintain and improve their professional competence** and strive to improve that of other professionals.

## The Seven Standards (memorize structure)

| **I** | **Professionalism** | A Knowledge of the Law · B Independence & Objectivity · C Misrepresentation · D Misconduct · **E Competence** |

## 2024 Revisions to the Code & Standards (high-yield — new vs. older editions)
*(Official V10, LM1 "Summary of the 2023 Revisions.")*
1. **NEW Standard I(E) Competence** — Members and Candidates must **act with and maintain the competence
   necessary to fulfill their professional responsibilities.** Competence = the abilities, skills, and knowledge
   needed for the duties assumed; varies by role; it does **not** mandate any specific CE program, and a negative
   investment outcome does **not** by itself prove a lack of competence. (Trap: recommending an unfamiliar asset
   class — e.g., crypto — while still "learning on" clients violates I(E).)
2. **Revised V(B) Communication with Clients** — now also requires disclosing the **nature of the services
   provided and the costs to the client** associated with those services (in addition to the existing duty to
   disclose the investment process and material changes).
3. **Renamed/expanded VI(A) "Avoid or Disclose Conflicts"** — the duty is now to **avoid** conflicts where
   reasonably possible, and only **disclose** when avoidance is not reasonable (previously "Disclosure of
   Conflicts" — disclosure only). Disclosures must be **prominent, in plain language, and effective.**

## High-Frequency Application Points (all subsections)

### I — Professionalism
- **I(A) Knowledge of the Law**: follow the **stricter** of law vs Code/Standards; **dissociate** from
  violations; no general duty to report to authorities unless **law requires** it (reporting to CFA Institute
  is encouraged, not mandatory). "Distribution-area" laws can apply across borders.
- **I(B) Independence/Objectivity**: modest gifts from **clients** (with disclosure) may be OK; gifts/benefits
  from **third parties** to influence research are problematic; pay for **own** travel/lodging on issuer site
  visits; do not let buy-side/sell-side, IB, or fund-sponsor pressure bias research; manage credit-rating and
  manager-selection conflicts.
- **I(C) Misrepresentation**: no knowingly false/misleading statements; **no plagiarism** (attribute sources);
  no guaranteeing returns on risky assets; misrepresentation can be by **omission**. Outsourced/third-party
  research can be used but the member is responsible for its accuracy.
- **I(D) Misconduct**: bars dishonesty, fraud, deceit, or any act reflecting adversely on **professional**
  reputation, integrity, or competence (covers personal conduct affecting professional standing, e.g. fraud).
- **I(E) Competence** *(2024 — new)*: must **have and maintain** the abilities, skills, and knowledge required
  for the duties assumed. Acting on an unfamiliar product before becoming proficient violates I(E); remedy is
  CE/training/certification or recusal. Negative outcomes alone ≠ incompetence.

### II — Integrity of Capital Markets
- **II(A) MNPI**: do not act/cause others to act on **material nonpublic** information; **mosaic theory**
  (combining **nonmaterial** nonpublic + public items) is permitted; use **firewalls/information barriers**;
  selective disclosure by issuers does not make info public.
- **II(B) Market Manipulation**: no **information-based** (spreading false rumors) or **transaction-based**
  (rigging trades, marking the close, securing a controlling position to manipulate price) manipulation.

### III — Duties to Clients
- **III(A) Loyalty, Prudence, and Care**: clients' interests **first**; identify the actual client (for a
  pension, the **beneficiaries**, not the plan sponsor); **vote proxies** in clients' best interest; obtain
  **best execution**; soft dollars must benefit the client.
- **III(B) Fair Dealing**: disseminate recommendations/changes to all clients **fairly** (not "equally");
  limit the people who know a change before dissemination; pre-disseminate allocation procedures for IPOs/hot issues.
- **III(C) Suitability**: in an **advisory** relationship, make a reasonable inquiry into the client's
  experience/objectives/constraints, **update the IPS regularly**, judge suitability in the **total-portfolio**
  context; for a stated **mandate**, only act consistently with it; document/caution on unsolicited off-IPS trades.
- **III(D) Performance Presentation**: presentations must be **fair, accurate, and complete**; no cherry-picking
  accounts/periods; adhering to **GIPS** is one way to comply. (Link → [[GIPS]].)
- **III(E) Preservation of Confidentiality**: keep client info confidential **unless** (1) it concerns the
  client's **illegal activities**, (2) disclosure is **required by law**, or (3) the client **permits** it.
  Confidentiality yields to a legal/regulatory (e.g., PCP) investigation.

### IV — Duties to Employers
- **IV(A) Loyalty**: act for the employer's benefit; **no soliciting clients before departure**; may **prepare**
  to compete; **skill/experience** and **public-source** info may go, **not** employer records/client lists;
  whistleblowing is allowed when it protects clients/markets and is not for personal gain.
- **IV(B) Additional Compensation Arrangements**: must get **written consent from all parties** before accepting
  outside comp/benefits (e.g., a client's performance bonus) that competes with the employer's interest.
- **IV(C) Responsibilities of Supervisors**: take **reasonable steps** to detect/prevent violations by those
  under supervision; an inadequate **compliance system** is itself a violation; decline supervisory duty in
  writing if you cannot discharge it. Detecting a violation → **investigate, increase supervision, limit
  activity** of the wrongdoer (not merely rely on assurances).

### V — Investment Analysis, Recommendations, and Actions
- **V(A) Diligence and Reasonable Basis**: have a **reasonable and adequate basis**; secondary/third-party and
  quant-model research must be vetted (understand model assumptions); group consensus you disagree with can be
  fine if it has a reasonable basis.
- **V(B) Communication with Clients** *(2024 — expanded)*: disclose the **nature of services and costs**, the
  **basic process/format and material changes**, and **significant risks/limitations**; **distinguish fact from
  opinion.** Identify factors important to the analysis.
- **V(C) Record Retention**: keep records supporting analysis/recommendations/actions; records are **employer
  property** (don't take them on departure); CFA Institute default suggestion is **7 years** absent a regulator rule.

### VI — Conflicts of Interest
- **VI(A) Avoid or Disclose Conflicts** *(2024 — renamed)*: **avoid** conflicts where reasonable, otherwise make
  **prominent, plain-language** disclosure (e.g., beneficial ownership, IB relationships, broker/manager
  arrangements, referral arrangements) to clients/prospects and employer.
- **VI(B) Priority of Transactions**: **client > employer > self** ("clients come first"); personal/beneficial
  accounts trade last; family fee-paying accounts are treated like any client.
- **VI(C) Referral Fees**: **disclose** any compensation paid/received for referrals to employer **and**
  clients/prospects **before** they engage, so they can judge the cost and any partiality.

### VII — Responsibilities as a CFA Institute Member or CFA Candidate
- **VII(A) Conduct as Participants in CFA Institute Programs**: no compromising exam **integrity/security**
  (don't reveal or solicit exam content, follow the Candidate Pledge); may express **personal opinions** about
  the program but not disclose confidential program information.
- **VII(B) Reference to CFA Institute / Designation / Program**: no misrepresenting the meaning of membership,
  the charter, or candidacy; **"CFA" is an adjective** (CFA charterholder), never a noun; cannot claim partial
  credit or imply superior performance from holding the charter; a candidate is one **currently** registered/sitting.

## Procedure / Recommendations (LOS 41.b)
Recommend **compliance procedures**: written **Code & compliance policies**; **restricted/watch lists** and
**firewalls** for MNPI; **personal-trading pre-clearance, reporting, and limits**; supervisory controls and an
adequate compliance system; **fair-dissemination** procedures; conflict **disclosure** and recordkeeping;
periodic competence/CE. Tie each recommendation to the specific Standard it protects.

## Exam Traps
- Choose the response demanding the **highest ethical standard**; partial compliance is still a violation.
- Follow the **stricter** of applicable law or the Code & Standards.
- **Mosaic theory** is allowed (not an MNPI violation); acting on material nonpublic info is not.
- Confidentiality (III-E) does **not** shield a client's **illegal** activity.
- **Standard I has FIVE subsections now** — don't forget **I(E) Competence** (added 2024).
- **VI(A)** is **"Avoid or Disclose"** (avoid first); "Disclosure of Conflicts" is the **old** title.
- **V(B)** now also requires disclosing the **nature of services AND the costs** to the client.
- A single fact pattern usually implicates **several** Standards — name **all** that apply, not just one.
- "CFA" is an **adjective** (CFA charterholder); using it as a noun violates **VII(B)**.

## Q&A

### 2026-06-03 — Stricter-law rule and the duty to dissociate
**Q:** A member works where local law is **less** strict than the Code & Standards. What governs, and must they report violations?
**A:** Under **I(A) Knowledge of the Law**, follow the **stricter** of applicable law or the Code &
Standards — so here the **Code & Standards** govern. If local law is *stricter*, follow the law. Members
must **dissociate** from known violations (stop participating, document, escalate). There is **no general
duty to report** violations to authorities **unless the law requires it** (reporting to CFA Institute is
encouraged but not mandated). Trap: "do nothing" or "just follow local law" is wrong when the Code is
stricter.
Related: [[GIPS]]

### 2026-06-03 — Mosaic theory, MNPI, and priority of transactions
**Q:** Two quick application points: is mosaic theory allowed, and who trades first?
**A:** **Mosaic theory is permitted** under **II(A)**: combining **nonmaterial nonpublic** information with
**public** information to reach a conclusion is fine — only acting on **material nonpublic** information
violates the standard. **VI(B) Priority of Transactions**: order is **clients first, then employer, then
self** ("clients come first") — personal/beneficial accounts trade last. Related **IV(A)**: on leaving an
employer you may take **skill/experience and public-source info** and prepare to compete, but **not**
employer records or client lists, and no soliciting clients before you depart.
Related: [[GIPS]]

### 2026-06-04 — The 2024 revisions: what changed in the Standards?
**Q:** What are the three changes introduced with the 2023/2024 revision of the Code & Standards that L2 candidates must know?
**A:** (1) **New Standard I(E) Competence** — members/candidates must **act with and maintain the competence
necessary** for their responsibilities (skills/knowledge appropriate to the role; no mandated CE program; a bad
outcome alone is not incompetence). (2) **V(B) Communication with Clients** was expanded to require disclosing the
**nature of services and the costs** to the client (on top of process/format, material changes, and risks).
(3) **VI(A)** was renamed **"Avoid or Disclose Conflicts"** — the duty is now to **avoid** conflicts where
reasonable and only **disclose** when avoidance is not reasonable, with disclosures that are prominent, plain-language,
and effective. Trap: Standard I now has **five** subsections (A–E), and "Disclosure of Conflicts" is the **outdated** VI(A) title.
Related: [[Application_of_the_Code_and_Standards]]

### 2026-06-04 — Worked case: recommending an unfamiliar asset (I(E), III(C), V(A))
**Q:** An adviser is excited about cryptocurrency but has never analyzed it. He buys a 1% crypto position for a few of his
**smallest** clients "to learn," intending to roll it out to everyone once he understands it better. Which Standards are at issue?
**A:** Multiple. **I(E) Competence** — he lacked the **knowledge/proficiency** in crypto when he made the initial buy,
so recommending it (and "learning on" clients) violates I(E); remedy is to gain competence via training/CE/certification
**before** recommending. **V(A) Diligence & Reasonable Basis** — without adequate research/understanding of the asset he
had **no reasonable basis** for the recommendation. **III(C) Suitability** — he must judge crypto's high risk against each
client's objectives/constraints and **total portfolio**, not impose it to satisfy his own curiosity; using a subset of small
clients also raises **III(B) Fair Dealing**. Exam point: at L2 the Application cases reward **naming every** Standard the
facts trigger, then stating the **required action** and a **firm policy** that would prevent recurrence.
